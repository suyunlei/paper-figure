## 2. The 7 Universal Design DNA Principles

1. **Neutral Architectural Ground (90% Rule)**: Both `#D9DBDE` studio grey (VoxCity) and `#FFFFFF` editorial white (Lei first-author papers) strip away background noise. Structural containers use hairline `0.8–1.6 pt` borders, `#F3F4F6`–`#E6E8EC` soft fills, or plain open whitespace—never saturated card backgrounds.
2. **One Focal Spatial Anchor per Figure**: Every strong figure centers on a tactile spatial representation (3D voxel stack, clay massing model, isometric city diorama, or multi-layer map) that establishes physical context before any algorithm box appears.
3. **Color Reserved Strictly for Scientific Meaning**: Buildings, terrain, and UI chrome stay grey/white/charcoal; color appears **only** on semantic layers, comfort classes, focal buildings (`#F0C419`), predicted routes (`#D6336C`), or GNN target nodes (`#D95F02`).
4. **Parallel Projection & Shared Camera Angles**: When multiple 3D stacks or graph planes coexist on one canvas, they share the same 30° isometric orientation so eyes traverse across stages effortlessly.
5. **Visual Layering via Restraint, Not Drop Shadows**: Hierarchy comes from line weight contrast (`1.6 pt` outer border vs. `0.9 pt` inner rail), solid grey header bands (`#D2D5DA`), dashed sub-zones, and white-halo `-30°` text—never fuzzy CSS/PowerPoint drop shadows.
6. **Circular Loupes & Dotted Leader Linking**: Street photos, zoomed heatmaps, and buffer details sit inside crisp circles rimmed in white or thin dark strokes and connect back to exact 3D points with fine dotted leaders and tiny dot anchors.
7. **Real Data Artifacts Inside Workflow Nodes**: Rather than drawing fake squiggles for results, UAL figures embed actual miniature SHAP bar charts, marginal-mean curves, attribute tables, and choropleth maps with north arrows and scalebars.

## 3. Explicit Anti-Pattern Contrast (What Never to Imitate)

One co-authored review diagram in the corpus (`2024-jag-geoai-hg`, *Mapping GeoAI in Quantitative Human Geography*) uses **glossy sky-blue and warm-yellow gradient PowerPoint rounded boxes with heavy outlines and dense paragraph text inside every box**. That style looks dated, low-precision, and immediately reads as generic presentation clip-art. Never use gradient-filled rounded boxes, 3D bevels, or multi-sentence text paragraphs inside nodes; always adhere to the crisp architectural vector aesthetics documented in Archetypes A–D.
